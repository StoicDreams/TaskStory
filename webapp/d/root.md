## How it works

<webui-sideimage src="/img/screenshots/problem.png" alt="A problem in Task Story, described with who, what, where, when, why, and how">

### 1. Define the problem

Start with what is wrong, not with how to fix it. Each problem has a summary and room for who has it, what it is, where and when it happens, why it matters, and how to reproduce it.

Keeping solutions out of this step leaves the team free to understand the problem before anyone commits to an answer.

</webui-sideimage>

<webui-sideimage reverse src="/img/screenshots/solutions.png" alt="Three solutions tagged with the same problem">

### 2. Try more than one solution

Add as many solutions as the problem deserves. Each one carries its own investigation, testing, and development notes, and is linked to the problem by a tag such as `problem:checkouttimeouts`.

Problems and solutions have an owner, so everyone knows who is driving each one.

</webui-sideimage>

<webui-sideimage src="/img/screenshots/publish.png" alt="Publishing a problem, with each solution marked as implemented or dropped">

### 3. Publish the story

When the work is done, publish the problem. Mark which solutions you implemented and which you dropped, check the preview, and Task Story turns it all into a single story.

The problem and its solutions leave your active lists, so the board only shows work that is still open.

</webui-sideimage>

<webui-sideimage reverse src="/img/screenshots/story.png" alt="A published story showing the problem, the integrated solution, and the dropped solutions">

### 4. Keep the history

Stories keep the problem, the solutions that shipped, and the solutions you dropped, with the notes that explain why.

Months later, when someone asks why it works this way or whether you ever tried something else, the answer is already written down.

</webui-sideimage>

## The method behind it

Task Story follows Continuous Agile Software Engineering (CASE), the way we work at Stoic Dreams: short iterations, problems before solutions, and learning from every decision, including the ones you dropped.
